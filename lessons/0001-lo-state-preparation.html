Two ways to prepare the same kind of topological order

The question to keep in mind: how do you make a local gauge constraint hold with certainty? One route measures its sign and corrects it. The other builds the right superposition coherently.

Learning aim: explain what changes between the two preparation methods in Lo et al., Universal gates from braiding and fusing anyons on quantum hardware.

Product state → Z₃ toric code → gauge charge conjugation → D(S₃)

A qutrit carries a three-valued label a; a qubit carries a two-valued label b. Charge conjugation reverses the qutrit label: C|a⟩ = |−a mod 3⟩. Thus |0⟩ stays fixed and |1⟩ exchanges with |2⟩. Promoting this global symmetry to a local gauge symmetry produces the S₃ quantum double. Theory, Appendix D.

A ground state obeys every vertex and plaquette projector: Aᵥ|ψ⟩ = Bₚ|ψ⟩ = |ψ⟩. These impose no electric charge and no local magnetic flux. On a torus, local constraints leave several possible logical sectors. Lo et al.’s pedagogical notes, §2.1.

What they actually did

Both experimental routes prepared Z₃ unitarily. The main route starts qutrits in |0⟩ and enforces vertices using fresh representatives in |+₃⟩ and controlled shifts CX/CX†. The adaptive comparison uses the dual plaquette construction, starting from |+₃⟩. Supplement, S6A.

Unitary · S6B2

Build each constraint

Choose a fresh |0⟩ edge qubit. Apply H, then use it to control X on three neighboring edge qubits and C on two qutrits. Repeat in the prescribed order.

The representative stays in the data register. The fresh-|0⟩ requirement creates the ordering dependency.

Adaptive · S6B1

Measure, then correct

Prepare auxiliary vertex qubits in |+⟩. Couple each to two qutrits by controlled C and neighboring |0⟩ edge qubits by CNOT. Measure vertex qubits in X; use the signs to choose edge-Z corrections.

The vertex ancillas are measured out. Corrections resolve the random signs.

Source: S6B, equations S56–S64 and Fig. 4. |+₃⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩ + |2⟩)/√3; |+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2.

Why the unitary replacement works

Here is a local algebraic derivation of the identity behind S64. Write the dressed vertex generator as S = XqT, where T acts on the remaining degrees of freedom and T² = I. Its desired projector is P₊ = (I + S)/2. Let |φ⟩ denote the rest of the state.

|0⟩q|φ⟩ → H on q → controlled T → |ψ₊⟩ = (|0⟩|φ⟩ + |1⟩T|φ⟩)/√2

Apply S to this expression. X swaps |0⟩ and |1⟩; T turns T|φ⟩ back into |φ⟩. The two terms exchange, so S|ψ₊⟩ = |ψ₊⟩. You have prepared the +1 eigenspace directly.

An ancilla measurement would instead give a branch proportional to (I ± S)|0⟩|φ⟩. The minus branch has a minus sign between the same two terms; Z on q removes it. The fresh |0⟩ qubit makes these branches equally weighted and supplies the control used by the unitary replacement.

This proves one local preparation step. Preparing the whole lattice also needs a legal ordering that preserves the constraints already established. The experiment supplies that ordering in Fig. 4.

Make the correction yourself

Z anticommutes with the X in an incident vertex generator. One edge-Z therefore flips two adjacent vertex signs. Along a path, interior vertices flip twice; only the endpoints change. This is the sign arithmetic behind pairing unwanted charges. Preparation theory, Appendix D.

What to remember when comparing them

The adaptive experiment used a different initial logical state: a Z₃ X-logical eigenstate, whereas the main preparation chose trivial flux. Its fidelity estimate concerns the ground-state manifold. Extended Data Fig. 2.

Parallel ancilla preparation and measurement permit bounded quantum depth for gauging. The unitary dependency chain grows with lattice length. A fully constant-depth preparation would also need an adaptive Z₃ stage, unlike the implemented comparison. Classical communication and available ancillas matter when turning quantum depth into elapsed time. Theory, Appendix D; S6A–C.
